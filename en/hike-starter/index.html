Honest and short

How I got into it.

I wasn’t born into it — I was taken along.

It started with my dad and my grandpa, who often took me up the mountains as a kid. I’m still really grateful for that today. These days I mostly go with friends who share the same hobby.

And that’s exactly the best part for me: enjoying nature together, as two or in a small group. You don’t have to figure it out alone. Bring someone — the mountain is best with two.

My picks

Why exactly these three.

A good, varied mix: one with a hut and a postcard view, a quiet lonely one to unwind, one with a wide view over the region. All three are very doable. Start with the one closest to you.

i.

Grünstein 1.304 m · Berchtesgaden

Hut, postcard view, Königssee

For me one of the most beautiful views so far — and the trail is really relaxed. At the top the Grünsteinhütte is waiting with food and drinks. And if you feel like more, keep going to the Archenkanzel: the best view over the Königssee I know.

Sebi’s note

The Grünstein also has a via ferrata — that one is NOT for beginners. Take the normal trail 445, it has no tricky spots. The Archenkanzel is another ~1.5 h further, so plan it as a full day.

ii.

Zinnkopf 1.227 m · Chiemgau

Lonely, summit cross, to unwind

It started as a spontaneous hike of mine, I’ve been up twice now. A very lonely summit — beautiful wooden cross, benches, a relaxed spot. There are several starting points, pick the closest one. Perfect to get started: easy, wooded, no crowds.

Sebi’s note

The lower part can get properly muddy and wet — sturdy shoes. There’s no hut on the mountain itself, so pack a snack. And honestly: the best view is already halfway up, not only at the top.

iii.

Dürrnbachhorn 1.776 m · Chiemgau

Wide view over the region, mountain-pine ridge

I always start from the Winklmoosalm car park. At the top you get a good view over the whole region, lots of mountain pines along the way. Often doable in spring and still in autumn — the high start makes it possible.

Sebi’s note

The Winklmoosalm road is a toll road — check the current price on site. There’s even an old nostalgic chairlift going up, if you want it easy. The ridge beyond the summit needs sure footing — to get started, just go up and back the same way.

Before you set off

What really comes along.

In the backpackThat’s all you need

  • Sturdy hiking boots with good tread — no sneakers
  • Poles — the ones from Alpin Loacker are honestly goat
  • Sunglasses + sunscreen + cap — the sun hits hard up top
  • At least 1.5 L of water + a snack
  • Rain jacket + a warm layer — mountain = changing weather
  • Mini first-aid kit + blister plasters + phone with an offline map

In your headThe more important gear

  • Good mood — you’re bringing that anyway, right?
  • Stamina — it comes by itself, a little more every time
  • Respect for the mountain and nature — the mountain isn’t going anywhere, better turn back if things turn
  • Openness — everyone’s friendly in the mountains, for real
